# Blueprint — Affiliate / CPA arbitrage business (offer-first)

> **How this type differs from `content`/`youtube`.** Those start from an owned audience or asset and affiliate revenue is one lever on top. **This type starts from the offer**: select a converting affiliate or CPA offer, acquire suitable traffic, and keep the spread. The asset is the **funnel economics plus tested creative**, not an audience or product. It is paid-media-native, capital-intensive, and structurally fragile: offers decay and ad accounts can be restricted. This blueprint is research-grounded rather than systematically operated.

---

## 1. The model in one screen

You do not own the product or the audience. You (1) select a converting offer on a third-party network, (2) buy or generate the cheapest traffic that fits it, (3) keep the difference between traffic cost and commission. The whole business reduces to one inequality: **EPC (earnings per click) must exceed CPC (cost per click), with enough margin to fund the losing tests.** Most offers and most creatives lose money, so this only works as a **disciplined portfolio with a tracker, fast kills, real compliance, and diversified accounts**, never as a single campaign, and never as a passive asset. A winning campaign is a **depreciating asset** (offers get capped/pulled, creatives fatigue, accounts ban, conversions get clawed back for refunds/fraud weeks later), not an annuity.

**The tension that IS the model:** the offers that convert best (nutra/supplements, sweeps, biz-opp, dating) are exactly the ones the safe, high-quality traffic platforms police hardest, which pushes you toward permissive, lower-quality traffic and toward the compliance edge. Manage that tension deliberately or it manages you.

## 2. Offer selection

**The networks split into structurally different camps, decide the camp first:**

| Camp | Networks | Fit for arbitrage |
|---|---|---|
| **Open direct-response marketplaces** | **ClickBank** (~6M products, up to 75% commission, 60-day cookie, weekly pay), **Digistore24** (EU-strong, ~8.5k vetted offers, 50-85%, 180-day cookie) | The arbitrage heartland, instant links, high-commission digital/nutra, built for paid direct-response. Digistore24 is the more vetted, ClickBank the larger/noisier. |
| **CPA / lead-gen networks** | **MaxBounty** and peers (pay per lead/install/sale/call) | Application + approval required; enforce fraud rules hard and can **reverse conversions post-hoc**. Good for lead-gen verticals (finance, insurance, sweeps). |
| **Curated brand networks** | **CJ Affiliate, Impact.com** | Mostly **NOT arbitrage-friendly**, enterprise/brand partnerships, 10-30% commissions, brands vet publishers and dislike paid arbitrage on their terms. This is the *legit-brand publisher* lane (overlaps `content`), not the buy-cheap-traffic lane. |
| **LATAM / Brazil** | **Hotmart, Monetizze** | The regional analogues for PT/ES digital products. *Their concrete 2025 terms weren't verified in the research pass, do a specifics pass before claiming numbers.* Offer language must match the traffic geo. |

**The metrics to threshold on:**
- **ClickBank gravity** = a weighted count of distinct affiliates who made a recent sale (a competition/momentum signal, NOT your earnings). Community sweet spot **~50-150**: proven to convert without being brutally ad-fatigued. 200+ = saturated; a very low gravity is unproven, not automatically bad.
- **EPC > CPC** is the survival inequality. Watch both the network-reported average EPC (a starting signal) and **your actual EPC** once your traffic hits (the only one that counts), and check it **after refunds/reversals**, not before.
- **Payout size** buys test runway. Winning nutra/supplement offers show ~$120-200+ average payouts; a higher payout means you can absorb more non-converting clicks before you know if an offer works. Target **payout ≥ ~$100** so a test has resolution.
- **Refund rate.** High-commission direct-response = high refunds (ClickBank's 60-day money-back is standard), reversed sales silently destroy a "profitable" campaign. Check it before scaling.

**Verticals:** health/supplements/nutra is the #1-volume category (and simultaneously the most ad-policy-hostile); make-money-online, dating, sweeps, finance/insurance lead-gen, and iGaming convert but get policed hardest by the mainstream platforms (so they run on native/push). *The offers that convert best are the ones the safest traffic sources ban*, that tension IS the model.

## 3. Traffic economics (paid vs organic)

**Permissiveness is inversely correlated with traffic quality.** Which platforms allow vs effectively ban affiliate/CPA offers (this is where accounts die):

| Traffic source | Raw affiliate link? | Reality |
|---|---|---|
| **Google Ads** | No, needs an intermediate value page | Workable but strict: requires "useful, unique, original" content; thin bridge pass-throughs get flagged. High intent, high quality, high compliance bar. |
| **Meta (FB/IG)** | No raw links in ads, route to your own page | High ban risk for aggressive direct-response; Meta is suing cloakers and moving toward verified-advertiser-only revenue. Organic posting (with disclosure) is allowed; paid is restricted. |
| **TikTok Ads** | No raw links, needs a landing page | Cheap reach, suspension-prone for direct-response. |
| **Native (Taboola, Teads)** | **Yes, affiliate-friendly** | **The workhorse** of offer-first arbitrage (the classic nutra/advertorial channel). CPCs ~$0.25-0.90. *Note: Outbrain merged into Teads in 2025, "Outbrain" standalone is gone.* |
| **Push / Popunder (PropellerAds, RichAds)** | **Yes, most permissive** | Cheapest (CPC from ~$0.005), lowest-quality, needs heavy fraud filtering. Where the banned verticals go. |

**The arbitrage math to state plainly:** because most offers/creatives fail, a portfolio needs winners at roughly **2-3x ROI (EPC ≈ 2-3x CPC on winners)** to pay for the dead tests. A campaign at a razor 1.1x ROI can't fund the testing pipeline and dies on the first offer-expiry or refund spike.

**Organic paths** (SEO content, short-form video, Reddit) are slower, cheaper, more durable, and less bannable, but the moment you build an owned content asset you've drifted from pure offer-first arbitrage *toward* the `content`/`youtube` model. Keep the type boundary crisp: offer-first purists use paid; the organic version is really a content business that monetizes via affiliate offers.

## 4. Funnel

Three shapes, in order of aggressiveness/risk:

1. **Direct-linking** (ad → offer's sales page). Fastest, but **banned by Google and Meta** and disliked by many networks; survives only on permissive push/pop and some native. Worst tracking, highest ban risk.
2. **Bridge / pre-lander / advertorial** (ad → your intermediate page → offer). **The standard arbitrage funnel.** The bridge exists for three reasons: **compliance** (satisfies "no direct affiliate link" + Google's original-content bar), **warm-up/conversion** (pre-sells + qualifies so the offer's own page converts better), and **tracking** (where your pixel/postback fires, so you can attribute + optimize by placement/creative).
3. **Full value page** (real reviews, comparisons, calculators). Required to run on Google; slowest to build, most durable, lowest ban risk.

**Allowed-by-whom:** direct-link → push/pop/some native only; bridge/advertorial → native + push, tolerated-if-genuinely-valuable on Meta; full value page → required for Google, safest on Meta. Build the funnel to the *strictest* platform you intend to run.

## 5. Compliance & risk (the part that ends businesses)

- **FTC disclosure (US).** Any material connection must be disclosed **clearly, in plain English, placed next to the endorsement** (a bottom-of-page line is insufficient), **every time** it appears. 2025-26 enforcement targets fake reviews, undisclosed incentives, and manipulated testimonials, which is *exactly* what advertorial arbitrage tends to produce. This is a live risk, not a formality (penalty ceiling cited ~$43,792 per violation).
- **Ad-account bans** are constant and treated by the community as a cost of doing business, that alone tells you how fragile this is. Diversify accounts.
- **Network bans / clawbacks.** Networks terminate for fraud, traffic manipulation, fake leads, incentivized traffic, or ToS-violating claims, and can **hold or reverse conversions post-hoc** for fraud/duplicates/chargebacks. You can be "profitable" on the dashboard and still get clawed back.
- **Cloaking, the critical distinction.** **URL cloaking** (hiding an ugly affiliate URL behind a clean branded short link) is universally allowed. **Ad cloaking** (showing the review bot a clean page and real users a different one) is **explicitly banned**, near-100% detected within ~30 days, and triggers **permanent bans**. It is the community's worst-kept secret and a landmine, **a sustainable build does not depend on it.**

## 6. Economics / kill criteria

- **Test-and-scale, tracker-driven.** Small fixed test budget per offer/creative → collect data → data-driven kill or scale. Use a tracker (Voluum, CPV Lab) to attribute by placement + creative and auto-kill losers, no tracking = flying blind = guaranteed loss.
- **Kill threshold:** the widely-cited rule is **no conversion after ~3x the payout in spend → kill** the offer/angle (block placements that spend 2-3x your target CPA with zero conversions). On a $150-payout offer that's ~$150-450 risked before an angle is declared dead.
- **Don't kill too early either.** Native-ads guidance suggests ~50-100 conversion events per variant for real optimization conclusions. The payout size sets the resolution, cheap offers give data faster but less margin. Balance protecting capital against having signal.
- **Realistic ceiling:** winners commonly run ~1.5-3x ROI; a first "profitable" campaign might net only ~$50/day. That's a foothold you scale, not a jackpot. Verify actual EPC > CPC *after* refunds/reversals.

## 7. Launch Roadmap (Week 1 → Week N)

**Relative weeks**, schedule them into the owning unit's `tasks.md` when you commit.

| Week | Focus | Concrete output |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| **W1** | **Pick network + offer** | Choose one network matched to geo (ClickBank/Digistore24 global digital/nutra; MaxBounty CPA lead-gen; Hotmart/Monetizze BR/LATAM, verify specifics first). Pick ONE offer that thresholds: gravity ~50-150, healthy network EPC, **payout ≥ ~$100**, sane refund rate. Read the network ToS + the offer's allowed traffic sources. **Gate: an offer that clears the thresholds, or keep looking.** |
| **W1-W2** | **Build a compliant funnel** | Build a bridge/pre-lander/advertorial or a real value page (via the `frontend-design` skill, Tailscale-only). Model structure on proven pages but **write your own compliant copy**, no cloaking, no fake claims, FTC disclosure next to every endorsement. Match the funnel type to the traffic source's rules (§4). |
| **W2** | **Tracking** | Stand up a tracker (Voluum/CPV Lab); wire the offer's postback/pixel; tag every placement + creative so you can kill at the granular level. |
| **W2-W3** | **Small traffic test** | Start on a permissive, cheap source that fits the offer (native for nutra/advertorial; push/pop for sweeps/dating; Google/Meta only with a real value page). Budget the test to the kill rule (~1-3x payout per angle); run several creative angles, not one. |
| **W3-W4** | **Kill or scale** | Kill any angle/placement past ~2-3x payout with zero conversions. On anything at positive ROI *after refunds*, scale spend gradually, add geos/placements, refresh creative before fatigue. **Gate: a verified EPC > CPC winner, or move on.** |
| **W4+** | **Systematize** | Turn the one winner into a repeatable pipeline: spy → clone-and-improve funnel → track → test → kill/scale, cycling new offers continuously (winners decay). Diversify ad accounts + offers to survive the inevitable bans and offer-deaths. Build the **machine**, not the campaign. |

> Not everything fits a week-by-week plan, the offer/traffic/compliance/kill criteria are reference throughout. The roadmap is the **build sequence**; the rest is the **operating manual**.

## 8. Kill / scale criteria

- **Kill an offer/angle** after ~3x payout in spend with zero conversions, or once actual EPC (post-refund) sits below CPC with no creative/placement fix in reach.
- **Scale** a campaign only on a positive-ROI winner verified after refunds/reversals, gradually (add geos/placements, refresh creative before fatigue), never all at once.
- **Kill the whole play** for yourself if you can't stomach permanent account bans, capital burn on dead tests, and post-hoc clawbacks, this model is high-variance by construction.

## 9. Common mistakes

1. **Direct-linking on Google/Meta**, instant disapproval/suspension; use a value page or bridge.
2. **No tracker**, you can't kill what you can't attribute; flying blind guarantees loss.
3. **Reading network EPC as your EPC**, the only number that counts is your own, after refunds.
4. **Scaling before refunds land**, a "profitable" dashboard reverses weeks later.
5. **Building on ad-cloaking**, near-100% detected in ~30 days, permanent ban; not a strategy.
6. **Advertorial fake claims / missing FTC disclosure**, exactly what enforcement targets in 2025-26.
7. **One offer, one creative, one account**, a portfolio with fast kills + diversified accounts is the only durable shape; single-anything dies on the first ban or offer-death.
8. **Treating a winner as an annuity**, it's a depreciating asset; keep the offer pipeline running.

## Phases

The manual runs in four phases: the stages an offer-first arbitrage operation walks, from a chosen offer to a portfolio that survives the bans and offer-deaths built into the model. Each capability below sits in the phase where it becomes the focus, and a unit's check shows which phase it is completing. Phases gate on verified economics, not time — an unproven spread has not earned more spend.

- **Phase 1 · Pick the offer** — one network matched to geo and vertical, its terms and allowed traffic sources read, and one offer that clears the thresholds on payout, momentum, and refund rate. *Gate in: none, this is the entry. Focus: refuse offers that cannot resolve a test.*
- **Phase 2 · Build the funnel and the tracking** — a compliant bridge or value page built to the strictest platform in play, disclosure next to every endorsement, and a tracker wired to the postback so every placement and creative can be attributed. *Gate in: an offer that clears the thresholds. Focus: never spend a euro you cannot attribute.*
- **Phase 3 · Test, kill, scale** — several creative angles on a permissive source that fits the offer, losers killed at the threshold, and a winner verified after refunds and reversals. *Gate in: a compliant funnel with tracking live. Focus: the only EPC that counts is your own, after clawbacks.*
- **Phase 4 · Systematize the machine** — a continuous offer pipeline, creative refreshed before fatigue, and accounts and offers diversified, because a winner is a depreciating asset. *Gate in: a verified positive-ROI winner. Focus: build the machine, not the campaign.*
